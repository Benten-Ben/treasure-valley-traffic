# 14. UI v2: one map, every layer

The owner asked for a rebuild on Oct 6, 2026: a single map styled like a
city-builder (Cities: Skylines), where every layer can be switched on
together, with 3D buses, stops and cameras, and a much smoother app. This
chapter is the plan; [chapter 13](13-visual-design.md) still holds the
tokens, fonts and usability rules, and decisions go in
[DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).

## 14.1 What changes

| Today | v2 |
|---|---|
| Lenses: one data layer at a time (keys 2, 4, 7) | **One map; layers switch on and off together** from a toolbar of round "info view" buttons along the bottom, each with its own legend |
| Calibration is a separate page; going there and back rebuilds the map and resets the view | **One persistent map.** Calibrating, looking through a camera and every panel happen on the same map; the view is never lost |
| Camera images are fetched only when a camera is opened | **Live images:** recorded cameras show their newest captured frame and keep updating; any camera that's open, looked through or being configured is refreshed from 511 about once a minute |
| Buses jump to each new GPS fix (glide 2.5 s) | **Playback about 90 s behind live:** buses drive along their recorded path between fixes at the measured speed; a LIVE pill and speed controls later grow into full replay (ch. 13's game speed) |
| Buses and stops are flat circles | **Small 3D buses** in their route's color, facing their heading; **3D stop shelters** |
| 8 route colors; overlapping routes drawn on top of each other | **A larger validated palette** (12+), with the no-clash rule extended to "too similar"; **routes sharing a street drawn side by side** like a transit map |
| All routes at full strength | **Routes with no bus running now are faded** |
| Streets in 5 speed bins | **A continuous shade per speed** (20 → 65 mph) |
| No lane data | **Lane-level roads** where data allows: lane counts, turn lanes and where they start, estimated width |
| Cameras as map nodes; images draped on the ground | **3D camera models on poles** at the calibrated position, height, heading and tilt, with a view cone; **floating camera windows**; **"look through"** puts the map's own camera at the camera's pose so the image lines up |

## 14.2 One map, with layers

- **Bottom toolbar, Cities: Skylines style:** round icon buttons for Transit,
  Streets (speed limits), Lanes, Cameras, Road weather, and later Crashes,
  Work zones and Counts. Each toggles its layer; any combination works.
  - Hover shows a tooltip; a pressed button opens that layer's legend card
    above the toolbar.
  - Choices are remembered between visits.
- **Top bar:** the nameplate, live chips (buses running, cameras live,
  road-weather stations live), the clock and the LIVE / playback pill.
- **Clay mode:** while any data layer is on, the base map can fade to clay
  (one switch), as in ch. 13. With several layers on, each keeps its own
  visual channel: routes are colored lines, streets a single blue ramp,
  cameras and buses 3D objects.
- **Panels:** bus cards, camera windows and the calibrator float over the map
  and can be moved; none of them leaves the map.

## 14.3 Transit

- **Colors:** a larger palette (12 or more), checked for contrast on the
  cream map and for color-vision deficiency. Neighbor routes (sharing at
  least 300 m of street outside the hubs) must not get the same color or
  colors too close to tell apart. "Too close" becomes a measured color
  difference instead of a hand-made list. Existing colors are kept where
  they still pass.
- **Side by side:** where routes share a street, each gets its own slot,
  offset from the centerline, in a consistent order along the corridor, the
  way printed transit maps draw them. The slots are worked out once per
  schedule load on the server, not in the browser.
- **Active routes:** a route with a bus reporting in the last 15 minutes is
  drawn at full strength; the rest at about 30%.
- **Buses:** small low-poly 3D buses in their route color, turned to their
  heading, sitting on the terrain.
- **Stops:** small 3D shelters with a sign post, shown from about zoom 15.
- **Playback:** the map shows buses about 90 seconds behind live. Fixes come
  every 30 s and reach us about 4 s later, so the next fix is always known.
  Each bus moves along its trip's route shape between fixes at the speed
  implied by the distance and time, instead of straight across corners.

## 14.4 Streets and lanes

- **Speed shading:** one hue (blue), light to dark, continuous from 20 to
  65 mph, so each posted speed has its own shade. The legend stays a simple
  ramp with the common speeds marked.
- **Lane data available:**
  - **OpenStreetMap** has lane counts on nearly all highways, arterials and
    collectors in the valley (sample around Eagle: trunk 99%, primary 100%,
    secondary 89%), and turn lanes (`turn:lanes`) on 21–87% of them. Turn
    bays are mapped by splitting the road where the lane count changes, so
    where a turn lane starts is known. Width is almost never tagged.
  - **ITD's road inventory (HPMS)** has through lanes, lane width, medians
    and shoulders on state routes and major roads.
  - **ACHD's Master Street Map** has existing and planned lanes on
    arterials and collectors. ACHD's centerlines (already loaded) have no
    lane or width fields.
  - **ACHD's 3-inch aerial photos** show every lane line and arrow, for
    checking, and later for extracting lane geometry where OSM is missing.
- **Rendering:** from about zoom 16, roads with lane data are drawn at their
  estimated real width (lanes × 3.6 m plus shoulders), with lane lines and
  turn arrows where turn lanes are tagged; elsewhere, the speed-shaded line.

## 14.5 Cameras

- **3D cameras:** a pole at the calibrated position (or ACHD's point,
  dashed, if not yet calibrated), with a camera head at the solved height,
  turned to its heading and tilted down; a faint view cone shows what it
  sees. Hovering brightens the cone.
- **Click a camera** to open a **floating window** with its live image. You
  can drag it, resize it and keep several open.
- **Look through:** a button in the window flies the map's own camera to the
  camera's exact position, height, heading, tilt, roll and field of view
  (MapLibre 6 supports all of these). The live image fades in over the map
  and lines up with it. Moving away fades the projection out, so it never
  looks stretched from the wrong angle.
- **Not calibrated yet:** the window opens at ACHD's estimated location
  with a **Configure** button; calibration runs on the same map.
- **Live images:** cameras we record (34 key cameras, the road-weather
  views) show their newest captured frame, updated as frames arrive. Any
  other camera that's open, looked through or being configured is fetched
  from 511 about once a minute, only while it's open.

## 14.6 Performance

Measured causes come first (ch. 14 will record the before/after numbers).
Known or likely:

- **The map is rebuilt on every page change** (calibrate and back): the
  style, every tile and every layer reload and the view resets. v2 keeps one
  map for the whole session.
- **HTTP/1.1:** the server is plain HTTP inside the tailnet, so a browser
  opens at most 6 connections and map tiles queue up. HTTPS (a Tailscale
  certificate) would allow HTTP/2 and fetch them in parallel. That's a
  server change for the owner to approve.
- **Road tiles are cut by PostGIS on every request.** The roads change
  weekly, so the tiles can be built once into a file (or cached), with long
  cache headers.
- **Terrain is downloaded twice** (one source for the 3D surface, one for
  hillshading).
- **Short cache lifetimes:** map files are cached for an hour, though they
  only change when rebuilt. Versioned file names would let browsers keep
  them for good.
- **Everything loads up front:** layers, the calibrator and (later) 3D code
  should load only when switched on, then stay loaded.
- **Large API payloads:** route shapes, stops and camera lists should be
  small, compressed and cached by version.

## 14.7 Technology

- **deck.gl** (already in the stack, ch. 10) interleaved with MapLibre for
  the 3D models (`ScenegraphLayer`, glTF), view cones and side-by-side route
  ribbons. Loaded only when a 3D layer is on.
- **Models:** our own low-poly glTF models (bus, stop shelter, camera on a
  pole), made by a small script so their license is ours, tinted per route
  at run time.
- **Data:** new API endpoints for the newest frame of a recorded camera,
  route ribbons with slots, and bus tracks for playback.

## 14.8 Build order

1. **Foundation:** one persistent map, the layers toolbar, remembered view
   and layers, performance fixes that need no server change, faded inactive
   routes, the continuous speed ramp, live camera images.
2. **Transit:** the larger palette, side-by-side routes, playback along
   route shapes, 3D buses and stops.
3. **Cameras:** 3D poles and view cones, floating windows, look-through,
   calibration inside the map.
4. **Lanes:** OSM lane data into the database, lane-level rendering.
