# 3. Regional traffic context and data sources

This chapter covers how fast the region is growing, how congested it is and
where, the safety picture, major projects, and, most usefully for a research
team, which data you can actually get.

---

## 3.1 Growth

| Measure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Boise MSA population, July 2025 (Census) | **864,243**, up 12.2% from 770,194 in 2020 | Census Vintage 2025[^fredpop] |
| Boise MSA growth 2024→25 | **+2.2%**, 13th-fastest MSA nationally; Canyon County +2.9%, the fastest in Idaho | Idaho Dept. of Labor[^idol] |
| Ada + Canyon, 2026 (COMPASS estimate) | **876,760**: Ada 589,500, Canyon 287,260. Boise 256,450; Meridian 152,070; Nampa 126,810; Caldwell 82,770 | COMPASS, Apr 2026[^compasspop] |
| Forecast | **1.3 million by 2055** (Communities in Motion 2055 forecast, accepted Dec 2023; plan due by Dec 2027) | COMPASS[^cim2055] |

Growth near **2% a year** means traffic patterns shift enough to justify
retiming on many corridors every 1–3 years. That's much more often than the
national 3–5 year norm (see [chapter 4](04-improvement-playbook.md#43-evidence-retiming-pays-a-lot)).

## 3.2 How congested is it?

| Source | Boise result |
|---|---|
| **Texas A&M Urban Mobility Report** (2025 ed., 2024 data) | **47 hours** of delay per auto commuter (rank 66 of 101 urban areas), up from 28 in 2000, 35 in 2010 and 44 in 2019. Travel Time Index 1.22. Total 12.6M person-hours of delay.[^umr] |
| **TomTom Traffic Index** (2025) | ⚠️ #34 of 91 US cities; 34.7% average congestion; 47 hours lost in rush hour[^tomtom] |
| **ACS mean commute** | Boise MSA 23.8 min (2024). Ada County rose from 19.4 to 21.2 min (2005–09 → 2020–24); Canyon County from 24.4 to 25.3 min[^acs] |
| **COMPASS 2024 Congestion Management report** | 22% of interstate/state highway miles moderately or highly congested. 4.9 annual peak hours of excessive delay per capita. Caldwell→Boise on I-84 averages 31 minutes in the AM peak.[^cms2024] |

**Context:** Boise isn't among the most congested US metros by these
measures; it ranks mid-pack among midsize areas. The pain comes from **how
fast it's getting worse**, and from congestion concentrated on a handful of
corridors with few alternatives.

Most of these are **signalized arterials** feeding interchanges, or
segments where signals meet freeway ramps. That's where signal timing and
ramp coordination matter most, and where ACHD↔ITD or Nampa↔Caldwell↔ITD
seams sit.

**Fastest volume growth (2020–24):** SH-44 (about 8% a year), Chinden west of
Five Mile (7%), and I-84 in Canyon County (6–7%).[^cms2024]

### A local proof point: Nampa's 2025 Middleton Road retiming

COMPASS's 2024 congestion report includes a "Strategy Highlight" on this.[^cms2024]

- In 2025 the City of Nampa found "degrading performance and heavy PM peak
  hour queuing" on southbound Middleton Road (Laster St → Roosevelt Ave).
- Nampa made a signal timing modification, and COMPASS measured the result
  with INRIX travel-time data.
- COMPASS reported "significant improvements in average travel times … and
  travel time reliability during the PM peak." It concluded that
  "low-cost operational adjustments can have significant impacts on
  congestion."

The full loop (spot a problem, retime, measure with probe data) has already
been shown to work locally. The question is doing it systematically.

## 3.4 Safety

| Year | Ada crashes | Ada fatal | Canyon crashes | Canyon fatal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 7,231 | 21 | 3,826 | 21 |
| 2020 | 5,530 | 17 | 3,244 | 22 |
| 2021 | 6,769 | 21 | 4,293 | 26 |
| 2022 | 6,681 | 19 | 4,231 | 16 |
| 2023 | 7,081 | 31 | 4,268 | 29 |
| 2024 | 7,284 | 29 | 4,409 | 21 |

Source: ITD Office of Highway Safety.[^itdcrash]

COMPASS's **Regional Safety Action Plan** (2025, analyzing 2018–22) found:[^rsap]

- 1,904 fatal or serious-injury crashes.
- **36.8% were angle or turning crashes**, mostly at intersections.
- Large **four-leg signalized intersections with five-lane major roads**
  correlate most with these crashes.
- High-crash intersections include Amity & Meridian, Overland & Locust Grove,
  Lake Hazel & Meridian, Victory & Meridian, State & 15th, Pine & Meridian,
  2nd St S & Northside (Nampa), Midland & Cherry (Nampa), and Blaine & 21st
  (Caldwell).

Timing affects safety too: yellow and all-red intervals, protected vs.
permissive left turns, flashing yellow arrows, and leading pedestrian
intervals. This opens **HSIP** safety funding as well as congestion funding
(see [chapter 4](04-improvement-playbook.md#411-funding-sources)).

## 3.5 Major projects and transit

- **SH-16 extension** (I-84 → SH-44, limited access): whole corridor on track
  to open in **2027**.[^sh16] This will shift volumes on Chinden, Ten Mile,
  Linder and Star, so signal timing on those corridors will need revisiting
  afterward.
- **I-84 Centennial Way → Franklin Rd (Caldwell)** widening and new 10th Ave
  interchange: finishes 2027.[^i84]
- **Chinden West:** the last interim segment (Middleton → Star) "remains
  unfunded."[^chinden]
- **Eagle Rd variable speed limit pilot** (Fairview → Chinden): live since
  Aug 2026, two years.[^vsl]
- **ACHD Five-Year Work Plan 2026–30:** 266 projects, about $1.2B, published
  as GIS data.[^fyplayer]
- **Transit:** Valley Regional Transit has no dedicated funding source and
  depends on voluntary local contributions.[^vrthistory]
  - Nampa cut, then partly restored, VRT funding in July 2026; service cuts
    took effect Oct 1, 2026.[^vrtnampa]
  - Ridership was about 1.05M trips in 2025, vs. 1.37M in 2019.[^ntd]
  - Weak transit means the arterial signal network carries nearly all trips.

### Available through an agency sponsor or partner

| Source | What's in it | How |
|---|---|---|
| **NPMRDS** (federal probe speeds on the National Highway System) | 5-minute speeds and travel times | Available to state DOTs and MPOs. Request analysis from or partner with **COMPASS** or **ITD**. |
| **ITD's purchased arterial travel-time data** (INRIX) | Arterial speeds (the "Tier 2" network) | Used by COMPASS for congestion reports and the Nampa retiming evaluation; not public. Partner with COMPASS. |
| **COMPASS travel demand model** | Regional 4-step model with 2,489 traffic zones | Model runs by request; non-members are charged.[^tdm] |
| **ACHD signal data** (timing sheets, Centracs event logs, detector configuration, turning-movement counts, count values) | The actual signal performance data | Public records request (below) or a data-sharing agreement |

### Commercial probe data

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| **INRIX** (Signal Analytics, Roadway Analytics) | Quote-based pricing. Already in use in the region through ITD/COMPASS. |
| **Iteris ClearGuide**, **StreetLight**, **Replica**, **HERE** | Quote-based; no academic pricing found |
| **TomTom** | Traffic Flow API free tier (20,000 requests/month), but **evaluation use only, with no storing of results** under its terms; MOVE has a 30-day trial[^tomtomdev] |
| **Google Routes API** | 5,000 free requests/month at the Pro tier, then $10 per 1,000[^googleroutes]. Its terms **prohibit storing or bulk-downloading** directions results, so it **can't** be used to build a travel-time dataset. Google's agency product, Roads Management Insights, can (see [chapter 7](07-diy-data-collection.md)). |
| **Waze for Cities** | Free two-way data exchange for *public agencies*. We couldn't confirm whether ACHD, ITD or Boise participate.[^waze] |

### Public records (Idaho Public Records Act)

- **Timelines:** residents must get a response within **3 working days**,
  extendable to 10.[^pra103]
- **Fees:** the first 2 hours of labor and first 100 pages are free for
  residents.[^pra102]
- **Exemption to expect:** critical-infrastructure records whose disclosure
  could "jeopardize the safety" of people or property, including computer and
  telecom systems.[^pra105] Frame requests around **operational data**
  (timing plans, coordination patterns, detector configuration, event-log
  exports, count data, last-retimed dates), not network or security
  details.
- **Request existing records** rather than asking the agency to create new
  analyses.
- **Portals:** [ACHD public records request](https://www.achdidaho.org/about-achd/public-records-request)
  · [ITD public records](https://itd.idaho.gov/public-records/)

### Academic partners

- **Boise State University, Advanced Transportation Management Laboratory**
  (directed by Dr. Mandar Khanal). It has "a fiber optics connection to
  Traffic Management Center located at the premises of the Ada County
  Highway District headquarters." That makes it the most natural partner for
  research access to ACHD data.[^bsu]
- **University of Idaho NIATT** (National Institute for Advanced
  Transportation Technology). University of Idaho researchers evaluated
  ACHD's 2017–19 connected-vehicle pilot.[^niatt]

## 3.7 Recommended first data steps

1. **Pick corridors** from the COMPASS top-10 list (§3.3) and ACHD's unfunded
   retiming and performance-measures list ([chapter 2](02-treasure-valley-signal-system.md#25-whats-on-the-unfunded-list)).
2. **Pull free baselines:**
   - ITD AADT and monthly volumes;
   - ITD crash points filtered to intersection-related crashes on those
     corridors;
   - COMPASS congestion measures.
3. **Collect your own travel times** with GPS floating-car runs. Commercial
   traffic APIs (Google, TomTom, HERE, Mapbox) don't permit storing their
   results long-term; see [chapter 7](07-diy-data-collection.md) for what is
   allowed and the tools in `tools/`.
4. **Log ACHD camera snapshots** at key intersections during peaks to
   document queues and phase failures, once ACHD has given permission (its
   image host disallows automated access).
5. **File records requests** with ACHD (and ITD for state routes) for timing
   sheets, last-retimed dates, detector trouble tickets and event-log
   exports.
6. **Approach COMPASS and Boise State** about probe-data analysis, mirroring
   the Nampa Middleton Road evaluation.
